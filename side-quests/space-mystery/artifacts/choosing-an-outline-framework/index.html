Choosing an outline framework

31 January 2024 · 09:34·AI draft

Download this document ↓

For the junior writer working on a narrative that combines elements of psychological thriller, science fiction, and interpersonal drama set against the backdrop of space exploration, the "novel outline" approach is likely the most fitting. This is because:

  1. Flexibility: The novel outline allows for a more flexible structuring of complex narratives, which can accommodate the detailed character development, intricate plot twists, and thematic depth your story demands.

  2. Complexity: Given the multiple layers of your story—ranging from the psychological journey of the protagonist to the geopolitical intrigue and the scientific challenges of space exploration—a novel outline can help manage this complexity effectively. It provides a structured yet adaptable framework to plot out these elements coherently.

  3. Character Depth: This approach supports deep character exploration and development, essential for your diverse cast of characters. Each character's backstory, motivations, and evolution can be intricately planned within the broader narrative.

  4. Thematic Exploration: A novel outline is conducive to exploring broad themes—such as ethics in space exploration, the impact of isolation on mental health, and international cooperation versus conflict—allowing you to weave these themes throughout the narrative in a nuanced manner.

While other outline types like "The Hero's Journey" or "Hollywood Beats" offer valuable frameworks for storytelling, they tend to follow more specific patterns that might constrain the multifaceted story you aim to tell. "The Hero's Journey," for instance, is centered around a singular protagonist's quest, which might not fully accommodate the ensemble cast and complex plot you envision. Similarly, "Hollywood Beats" is tailored more towards screenplay structure, focusing on visual storytelling that may not delve as deeply into the internal states and psychological complexities of characters as a novel would.

"Story Circle" and "Romance Outline" provide useful narrative structures but may be too specific or genre-focused for the broader scope of your story. The "Story Circle" is great for character development and transformation but might not provide enough guidance on managing the intricate plot and thematic layers. Meanwhile, the "Romance Outline" focuses on relationship dynamics and might not suit the broader thematic and plot-oriented ambitions of your story.

Ultimately, a novel outline offers the most comprehensive and flexible approach, allowing you to develop your richly layered narrative with its unique blend of genres and themes.
