# Chapter one — early prose version

1 - 2

The Kyiv sun dipped below the horizon, casting an orange glow onto the cobblestone streets. Thomas Harrington, a 45-year-old British diplomat with two decades of experience in intelligence, stood on the balcony of his apartment, overlooking the vibrant city that he now called home. He sipped his lukewarm tea and sighed, lost in thought about the intricate web of politics and subterfuge that defined his life.

"Another day, another diplomatic dance," Thomas murmured to himself as he drained the last of his tea and set the cup down on the small outdoor table. His daily routine was one of carefully orchestrated meetings, attending diplomatic functions, liaising with local officials, and subtly gathering information for MI6. An expert at concealing his true intentions, Thomas navigated this delicate world with practised ease.

The morning began with a breakfast meeting at a luxurious hotel, where Thomas exchanged pleasantries with Ukraine's Deputy Foreign Minister. Between bites of buttery croissants and sips of strong coffee, they discussed trade agreements and cultural exchanges, but Thomas's mind was always probing for any useful nuggets of information he could relay back to London.

"Of course, we value our partnership with the United Kingdom," the Deputy Foreign Minister said, dabbing his mouth with a napkin. "But there are always those who seek to disrupt such relationships, aren't there?"

"Indeed," replied Thomas, his eyes narrowing ever so slightly. "We must be vigilant against any threats, from within or without."

The afternoon found him at the British embassy, poring over classified documents and composing reports to be sent back to MI6 headquarters. With each stroke of his pen, Thomas skilfully wove together a tapestry of knowledge – names, dates, locations, and more – which would help form a clearer picture of the complex geopolitical landscape.

"Thomas, have you spoken to our friend at the Ministry of Defence?" asked his assistant, handing him a stack of papers. "He hinted at some new developments regarding their military budget."

"Ah, yes," Thomas said, scribbling a note in the margin of the report he was working on. "I'll follow up with him this evening. Thank you, Natasha."

As the sun set, Thomas attended a cocktail party at a lavish residence, rubbing elbows with politicians, journalists and other members of Kyiv's elite. His ears were always open, listening for whispers of information that could prove valuable. It was like assembling a puzzle, with each piece fitting together to reveal a larger picture. He laughed at a joke told by a prominent businessman, all the while making mental notes of who was speaking to whom and what they might be discussing.

"Thomas, my friend!" exclaimed a well-dressed man as he approached, clapping him on the back. "It's been too long since our last meeting. How have you been?"

"Very well, thank you, Petro," Thomas replied, smiling warmly. "And how is your family?"

"Ah, they are well, thank you for asking," Petro said, taking a sip of his champagne. "But enough about me, I hear there have been some interesting developments in London lately..."

As the evening wore on, Thomas continued to expertly navigate these social waters, collecting information and building alliances. And as the stars began to appear in the night sky above Kyiv, he knew that another day had been a success – a small victory in the grand game of diplomacy and espionage in which he was both player and pawn.

3 - 4

The sun dipped below the horizon, casting an orange glow across Kyiv. Thomas stood on the balcony of his apartment, sipping a cup of black tea as he watched the city below. He mentally switched between Russian, Ukrainian, and French with ease, mulling over the various conversations he had throughout the day. It was this linguistic prowess that set him apart from his peers, allowing him to blend seamlessly into the diverse tapestry of Eastern Europe.

"Vous parlez français, Monsieur Harrington?" a voice called out from behind him. Thomas turned to see his colleague, Marie, leaning against the doorframe.

"Oui, bien sûr," he replied with a smile, switching effortlessly to French. "I've been studying it since I was a boy. And yourself?"

"Ah, yes. My mother was French, so it was my first language," Marie said, stepping out onto the balcony. She gestured to the cityscape below. "You know, your ability to navigate these languages is quite impressive. I've seen you charm your way through high-stakes negotiations without breaking a sweat."

Thomas chuckled. "Well, it's vital in this line of work, isn't it? One wrong word could mean the difference between success and failure."

"Very true," Marie agreed. "Speaking of which, have you made any progress on identifying potential local assets?"

"Indeed, I have," Thomas said, his eyes scanning the bustling streets below. "There's a young journalist named Olena who shows great promise. Her access to information and her understanding of the political landscape makes her an ideal candidate for recruitment. I've already begun building rapport with her."

"Excellent," Marie nodded. "Our superiors at MI6 will be pleased. Your keen eye for talent and deep knowledge of the region make you invaluable to our team, Thomas."

"Thank you, Marie," he replied modestly. "But it's not just the language and the politics that matter. It's looking beyond the surface, understanding what makes people tick. Everyone has a story, and if you can tap into that, they'll be more likely to trust you."

"Very insightful," Marie said, raising an eyebrow. "I think I still have much to learn from you."

"Ah, well, we all have our strengths," Thomas said with a shrug, taking a final sip of his tea. "I may be good at reading people, but your technical skills are unmatched. Together, we make a formidable team."

"Agreed," Marie responded, her eyes twinkling as she clinked her cup against his. "Here's to many more successful missions, my friend."

As their cups met with a soft chime, Thomas gazed out over the city once more, ready to face whatever challenges awaited him in the ever-shifting world of diplomacy and espionage.

5 - 6

"Thomas, would you mind if I ask you a personal question?" Marie inquired tentatively, stirring her cup of tea as they sat on the embassy terrace overlooking Kyiv's bustling streets.

"Go ahead," Thomas replied cautiously, his gaze fixed on the horizon, where the golden domes of St. Michael's Monastery gleamed in the evening sun.

"Have you ever lost someone you cared about during an operation? Someone you trusted?" she asked, her voice softened with empathy.

Thomas hesitated for a moment, his eyes clouding over with memories he'd rather forget. "There was an operation in Chechnya some years ago," he began slowly, his voice strained. "Things went sideways, and... we lost people. Good people."

"Was it your fault?" Marie pressed gently, sensing that there was more to the story.

"Perhaps not directly, but I've always felt responsible. And then there was a betrayal by someone I considered a friend," he admitted, his jaw tensing at the recollection. "It's not something I like to discuss."

"Of course, I understand," Marie conceded, reaching over to place a comforting hand on his arm. "I just wanted you to know that we all have our demons, Thomas."

"Thank you, Marie. It means a lot." Thomas forced a smile, though his expression remained pained.

"Are you alright?" she asked with genuine concern, noting the dark circles under his eyes and the way his fingers trembled slightly as he lifted his cup to his lips.

"Most days," he confessed quietly, taking a long sip of his lukewarm tea. "But sometimes the past creeps up on me, and I find myself seeking solace at the bottom of a bottle."

"Thomas, that's not healthy," Marie scolded gently, her brow furrowing with worry. "If you ever need to talk, I'm here for you."

"Thank you," he murmured, touched by her offer. "But it's not just the alcohol, Marie. My family has paid the price for my past mistakes as well. My relationship with my wife and children is... strained, to put it mildly."

"Have you considered therapy?" she suggested softly, her eyes searching his for any sign of resistance.

"I've thought about it, but with everything going on around us, there never seems to be a good time to focus on myself," he admitted, looking down at his hands, now gripping the teacup tightly.

"Sometimes, Thomas, the best thing we can do for those we care about is to take care of ourselves first. Don't forget that," Marie advised, squeezing his arm reassuringly before rising from her seat. "Now, let's get back to work, shall we? We have a mission to complete."

As Thomas nodded and stood up, ready to face the challenges ahead, he couldn't help but wonder if his past experiences would continue to haunt him, threatening to jeopardize both his career and the lives of those he cared about. But for now, the weight of responsibility settled back onto his shoulders, and he knew he needed to stay focused on the task at hand. With a deep breath, he followed Marie back inside, determined to prove himself worthy of the trust placed in him.
