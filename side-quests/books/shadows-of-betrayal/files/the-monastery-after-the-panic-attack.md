# The monastery — after the panic attack

The golden light of dawn spilled over the Kyiv skyline, casting long, ethereal shadows over St. Michael's Golden-Domed Monastery. The quiet murmur of early risers in the city provided a soft undercurrent to the silence that wrapped the ancient building. Inside, Thomas Harrington found himself before a row of flickering candles, their flame dancing in the pre-morning glow.

Kneeling on the cold stone floor, the full weight of his past seemed to press heavily on his broad shoulders. A reel of memories, of places and faces, actions and reactions, played unbidden behind his closed eyes. Images from the night before flickered in between the memories of a past life – the loud crash, the surge of panic, and Maria's worried face.

"Lord, give me the courage to confront my demons and the knowledge to learn from them," he muttered under his breath. Each word echoed within the thick, serene atmosphere of the church, swallowed by the sturdy stone walls.

He lost track of time in his introspection, the quiet solitude of the monastery providing a space for his thoughts to unravel. His silent reflection was interrupted when a gentle voice called out his name.

"Thomas?" Father Petrov emerged from the dimly lit corner of the church, worry etched on his face.

"Father, I didn't realize you were here," Thomas said, his voice echoing softly in the vast expanse of the church. He rose to his feet, dusting off his knees.

"Your plea was quite heartfelt," Father Petrov mused, walking over to join him by the candles. "What burdens you?"

Thomas hesitated, then decided to confide in the man who had offered him spiritual guidance since his arrival in Kyiv. The events from last night added a new layer of complexity to his already burdened conscience.

"My past weighs heavily upon me, Father. In my life, I've made decisions, took actions I'm not proud of," he admitted, his voice barely above a whisper. "And last night... there was an accident outside the bistro... it brought back some memories..."

Father Petrov nodded, understanding etched on his features. "Son, redemption is never a simple journey. Yet, it's imperative to bear in mind that absolution starts within. One must first forgive oneself before seeking forgiveness from others. You've been given a second chance, Thomas, to learn from your past and build a better future."

Thomas was about to respond when his mobile phone buzzed in his pocket, the abrupt sound shattering the solemnity of the moment. Apologetic, he glanced at the text message and felt his heart clench.

“I have to go, Father,” Thomas said, his words tumbling out in a rush. “It’s an emergency.”

Father Petrov crossed himself and said the blessing. “May God be with you, my son.”

Thomas thanked the priest before exiting the church, the morning sunlight momentarily blinding him. As he hurried away from the tranquil sanctuary of the monastery, his heart thundered in his chest, reflecting the turbulence of his thoughts and emotions. He knew the day ahead would be anything but ordinary, a stark contrast to the serenity of his morning spent in reflection and prayer.
