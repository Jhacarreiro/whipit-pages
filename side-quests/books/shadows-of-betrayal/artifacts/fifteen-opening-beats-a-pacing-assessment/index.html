Fifteen opening beats — a pacing assessment

AI draft. Surviving writing draft; alternative versions are retained.

Download text ↓

  1. Thomas Harrington's Arrival: This beat is straightforward and introduces our protagonist. This singular action—stepping off the plane—needs to be meaty enough to handle 600 words, which might be challenging. The risk is dwelling too much on physical descriptions without adding depth.

    Suggestion: Expand by hinting at Thomas's internal state or thoughts upon arriving in Kyiv. Maybe include brief flashbacks or hints about why he's there, making it more layered.

  1. Kyiv's Atmosphere: This beat has a lot of potential, but 600 to 1200 words on just the atmosphere might lean towards over-descriptiveness.

    Suggestion: Intersperse with Thomas's actions. Maybe he interacts with a street vendor, observes a cultural quirk, or has an experience that's unique to Kyiv.

  1. Thomas's Observation: This might overlap with the second beat. The risk here is redundancy.

    Suggestion: Consider merging beats 2 and 3. Thomas can observe Kyiv's atmosphere while also forming his impressions.

  1. Natalia's Introduction: This beat seems balanced. Introducing a key character can easily consume 600-1200 words with background, physical description, and context.

  1. Natalia's Passion: This beat can be merged with her introduction. It's natural to show her passion as she's introduced, rather than separating the two.

    Suggestion: As she approaches Thomas, perhaps she's interviewing locals or documenting something. This can seamlessly show her love for Ukraine and her dedication as a journalist.

  1. Dialogue: An essential beat. The challenge will be ensuring the dialogue is weighty, substantial, and doesn't drag.

    Suggestion: Incorporate subtext and tension. Maybe there are things unsaid, cultural nuances Thomas misses, or differing viewpoints on the conflict.

  1. Thomas's Curiosity: This seems an extension of beat 6. Both beats revolve around conversation and can loop into one another.

    Suggestion: Combine the beats. As they converse, Thomas can display his curiosity naturally.

  1. Kyiv's Richness: This can potentially loop back to beat 2 and 3, leading to over-description.

    Suggestion: Make this beat interactional. Perhaps Thomas and Natalia visit a landmark, and a specific incident occurs that pushes the story forward.

  1. City Exploration: This beat might risk redundancy with previous beats discussing Kyiv's atmosphere.

    Suggestion: Focus on their interactions with locals, extracting information and uncovering layers of the city that tourists don't see.

  1. Contrasting Kyiv: The description here risks overlapping with beats 2, 3, and 8.

    Suggestion: This contrast should come out through an incident or encounter. Maybe they witness a conflict-related incident which juxtaposes with the city's vibrant spirit.

  1. Ukrainian Emotion: This can become repetitive if not handled with care.

    Suggestion: Narrate a poignant incident or share a heartfelt story from a local, bringing out the emotions of the Ukrainians.

  1. Thomas's Appreciation: The risk here is being too introspective and stalling the plot's progress.

    Suggestion: Showcase this appreciation through action. Maybe Thomas tries a local custom, learns a Ukrainian phrase, or defends the culture in some way.

  1. Parting Ways: This beat serves to signal the end of the chapter and seems apt. Their bond and determination can be emphasized through reflection and final words.

  1. Anticipation and Intrigue: A necessary beat, but there's a risk of being too vague or too revealing.

    Suggestion: Perhaps introduce a mysterious character observing them, hint at some hidden information Natalia is reluctant to share, or have Thomas discover an odd clue.

  1. Transition: This beat can take up to 600 words, so ensure it's not abrupt.

    Suggestion: Maybe introduce the name "Viktor Dmytrovich" here, but shroud it in mystery, or hint at a lead that Thomas needs to pursue, driving the reader into the next chapter.

Overall Impression: The beats provide a clear structure. However, there's a risk of redundancy, especially concerning Kyiv's descriptions and atmosphere. Merging or redefining some beats will ensure each can be adequately expanded without looping or overreaching.
