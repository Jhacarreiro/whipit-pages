# Chapter one — the June opening

The Kyiv sun dipped below the horizon, casting an orange glow onto the cobblestone streets. Thomas Harrington, a 45-year-old British diplomat with two decades of experience in intelligence, stood on the balcony of his apartment, overlooking the vibrant city that he now called home. He sipped his lukewarm tea and sighed, lost in thought about the intricate web of politics and subterfuge that defined his life.

"Another day, another diplomatic dance," Thomas murmured to himself as he drained the last of his tea and set the cup down on the small outdoor table. His daily routine was one of the carefully orchestrated meetings, attending diplomatic functions, liaising with local officials, and subtly gathering information for MI6. An expert at concealing his true intentions, Thomas navigated this delicate world with practised ease.

The morning began with a breakfast meeting at a luxurious hotel, where Thomas exchanged pleasantries with Ukraine's Deputy Foreign Minister. Between bites of buttery croissants and sips of strong coffee, they discussed trade agreements and cultural exchanges, but Thomas's mind was always probing for any useful nuggets of information he could relay back to London.

The Deputy Foreign Minister, dabbing his mouth with a napkin, spoke of the importance of their partnership with the United Kingdom.

"However," he continued cautiously, "we must be aware that there are those who may try to disrupt such relationships, don't you think?"

Thomas's eyes narrowed subtly as he replied.

"Absolutely," he said firmly. "We have to remain on guard against any danger, both within and without."

The afternoon found him at the British embassy, poring over classified documents and composing reports to be sent back to MI6 headquarters. With each stroke of his pen, Thomas skilfully wove together a tapestry of knowledge – names, dates, locations, and more – which would help form a clearer picture of the complex geopolitical landscape.

"Thomas," his assistant began, offering him a stack of papers, "have you spoken to our friend at the Ministry of Defence? He had something about their military budget..."

Thomas looked up from the report he was working on and nodded.

"Ah, yes," he muttered, jotting something in the margin as he spoke. "I'll follow up with him this evening. Thanks, Natasha."

As the sun set, Thomas attended a cocktail party at a lavish residence, rubbing elbows with politicians, journalists and other members of Kyiv's elite. His ears were always open, listening for whispers of information that could prove valuable. It was like assembling a puzzle, with each piece fitting together to reveal a larger picture. He laughed at a joke told by a prominent businessman, all the while making mental notes of who was speaking to whom and what they might be discussing.

“Thomas!” exclaimed a sharply dressed man as he walked up, clapping him firmly on the back. ”It’s been ages since we last spoke! How have you been doing?”

“I can’t complain, Petro,” Thomas replied, grinning widely. ”And your family? All is well?”

Petro took a sip of his champagne before answering. ”Yes everybody is great. But enough about us--I hear London has been rife with exciting developments lately, eh?”

As the evening wore on, Thomas continued to expertly navigate these social waters, collecting information and building alliances. And as the stars began to appear in the night sky above Kyiv, he knew that another day had been a success – a small victory in the grand game of diplomacy and espionage in which he was both player and pawn.

The sun dipped below the horizon, casting an orange glow across Kyiv. Thomas stood on the embassy terrace, sipping a cup of black tea as he watched the city below. He mentally switched between Russian, Ukrainian, and French with ease, mulling over the various conversations he had throughout the day. It was this linguistic prowess that set him apart from his peers, allowing him to blend seamlessly into the diverse tapestry of Eastern Europe.

"Vous parlez français, Monsieur Harrington?" a voice called out from behind him. Thomas turned to see his colleague, Marie, leaning against the doorframe.

"Oui, bien sûr," he replied with a smile, switching effortlessly to French. "I've been studying it since I was a boy. And yourself?"

"Ah, yes. My mother was French, so it was my first language," Marie said, stepping out onto the balcony. She gestured to the cityscape below. "You know, your ability to navigate these languages is quite impressive. I've seen you charm your way through high-stakes negotiations without breaking a sweat."

Thomas chuckled. "Well, it's vital in this line of work, isn't it? One wrong word could mean the difference between success and failure."

"Very true," Marie agreed. "Speaking of which, have you made any progress on identifying potential local assets?"

"Indeed, I have," Thomas said, his eyes scanning the bustling streets below. "There's a young journalist named Olena who shows great promise. Her access to information and her understanding of the political landscape makes her an ideal candidate for recruitment. I've already begun building rapport with her."

"Excellent," Marie nodded. "Our superiors at MI6 will be pleased. Your keen eye for talent and deep knowledge of the region make you invaluable to our team, Thomas."

"Thank you, Marie," he replied modestly. "But it's not just the language and the politics that matter. It's looking beyond the surface, understanding what makes people tick. Everyone has a story, and if you can tap into that, they'll be more likely to trust you."

"Very insightful," Marie said, raising an eyebrow. "I think I still have much to learn from you."

"Ah, well, we all have our strengths," Thomas said with a shrug, taking a final sip of his tea. "I may be good at reading people, but your technical skills are unmatched. Together, we make a formidable team."

"Agreed," Marie responded, her eyes twinkling as she clinked her cup against his. "Here's to many more successful missions, my friend."

As their cups met with a soft chime, Thomas gazed out over the city once more, ready to face whatever challenges awaited him in the ever-shifting world of diplomacy and espionage.

Marie timidly asked Thomas, as they sat together on the terrace of the embassy enjoying their cup of tea. "May I ask you a personal question?"

Thomas kept his eyes on the horizon and replied cautiously, "Sure."

Marie hesitated for a moment, before mustering her courage to ask her question, her voice now softer. "Have you ever lost someone you cared about during an operation? Someone you trusted?"

Thomas was quiet. He seemed to be lost in thought, remembering something painful from his past. After a few moments, he spoke slowly.  "There was an operation in Chechnya some years ago," he told her. His voice was strained with emotion. "It didn't go according to plan, and... we lost people. People that I cared about."

He paused for a moment, taking a deep breath as if to steady himself. He looked Marie in the eye and continued. "We had gone undercover, posing as businessmen in order to gain access to certain areas that were off-limits to us. But it was difficult - the people of Chechnya had suffered so much hardship, and they were mistrustful of outsiders. It was hard to earn their trust."

Thomas shook his head slowly, memories of his time there flooding back into his mind. "We managed to make some contacts over time, but it was too little too late. We were betrayed by someone we thought we could trust, and that person cost us dearly. Many brave men and women lost their lives that day," he said sadly, closing his eyes to hold back tears.

"I'll never forget the faces of those I failed," he whispered hoarsely, disguising his grief with a sigh before continuing on in a more composed voice.

“Do you think it was your fault?” Marie asked softly, feeling something behind his words.

"In a way," he sighed heavily, his knuckles whitening around the mug. "It was a betrayal from someone I thought I trusted. It's not something I like to relive."

"I understand," she replied warmly, squeezing his arm in comfort. "We all have our hardships to bear, Thomas."

"Thanks, Marie." He attempted a wan smile. There was still a sadness lingering on his face.

"Are you okay?" she questioned kindly, noting the exhaustion in his features and the slight shaking of his hands as he sipped from his cup.

"Most days," he said in a low voice, stirring his lukewarm tea with a spoon. "But then I start to remember things I'd rather forget, and that's when I turn to the bottle."

Marie frowned, her face filled with concern. "Thomas, you can't keep doing that. If you want to talk, I'm always here for you."

He was touched by her kindness. "Thanks... it's not just the alcohol though. I've hurt my family too, and now our relationship is... complicated."

She nodded slowly. "Have you thought about seeing a therapist?" Her eyes were on him, waiting for an answer.

"I've considered it, but with everything that's happening there never seems to be a good time to focus on myself," he said, his stare sinking to the tea cup now tightly gripped in his hands.
"Sometimes, Thomas," Marie started as she patted his arm reassuringly before standing. "The best thing we can do for those we care about is to take care of ourselves first. Don't forget that. Now let's get back to work, okay? We have a mission to finish."

As Thomas nodded and stood up, ready to face the challenges ahead, he couldn't help but wonder if his past experiences would continue to haunt him, threatening to jeopardize both his career and the lives of those he cared about. But for now, the weight of responsibility settled back onto his shoulders, and he knew he needed to stay focused on the task at hand. With a deep breath, he followed Marie back inside, determined to prove himself worthy of the trust placed in him.
