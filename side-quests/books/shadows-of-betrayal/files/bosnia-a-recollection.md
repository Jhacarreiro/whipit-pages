# Bosnia — a recollection

"All right, Marie, fasten your metaphorical seatbelt, because you're about to take a detour down Memory Lane in the Wild, Wild East: Bosnia," I began, a mischievous glint in my eye.

"Picture us, a motley crew of stone-faced soldiers, stumbling into a small village teeming with life, right in the middle of their annual log-throwing festival. And no, I'm not talking about a friendly game of tossing twigs. These were real, honest-to-goodness, could-crush-you-if-you're-not-careful logs."

I chuckled as I saw her eyes widen in surprise. "Oh, you think that's funny? Imagine our shock. There we were, armed to the teeth, trained to expect danger lurking around every corner, and instead, we're met with villagers who couldn't wait to show us how far they could fling a piece of wood. It was like walking into a Monty Python sketch."

"But you know what the kicker is?" I asked, leaning in closer. "We didn't just participate, we actually won! Us, a bunch of grim-faced soldiers, out-throwing these villagers who had probably been log-tossing since they were knee-high to a grasshopper. The underdogs emerging victorious, imagine that!"

"As dusk settled and the stars began to twinkle overhead, we made our way out of the village. But not before being waved off by a sea of beaming faces, their smiles as bright as the banners fluttering in the night breeze. It was a moment of warmth and joy in a world that often seemed too cold and harsh."

"And there you have it, Marie," I concluded with a dramatic flourish. "That's the tale of how I went from dodging bullets to dodging logs. War is hell, but every now and then, life throws you a log and says, 'Here, have some fun with this.'"
