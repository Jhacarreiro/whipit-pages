# The final surviving opening-beat revision

1. **Arrival of Thomas Harrington**: As he steps off the plane in Kyiv, emphasize not only his physical features but also his unique habit of observing people's shoes—a quirk from his detective days. His worn-out brogues might hint at many miles and mysteries traveled.

2. **Kyiv's Dual Atmosphere**: Describe Kyiv's vibrancy, contrasting the bustling streets with subtle undertones of tension from the conflict. Maybe the shadow of a military plane overhead or a hastily taken-down protest sign in a shop window hints at the underlying unrest.

3. **Thomas's Kyiv**: As Thomas meanders, focus on his sensory experiences—the sights, sounds, even the unusual taste of local delicacies. Highlight his unease and the feeling of being an outsider, contrasting with memories of more familiar cities.

4. **Introduction of Natalia Ivanova**: When Natalia approaches, describe her not just physically but through Thomas's observations, maybe noting the ink stains on her fingers (indicative of her journalist role) and her well-worn yet proud posture. Have Thomas notice a distinctive pendant she wears—a hint of local folklore?

5. **Natalia’s Narratives**: As she talks about Ukraine, intersperse her dialogues with snippets of stories she's covered—like the lullaby sung during an airstrike. She could also share a chilling urban legend or a popular local tale, setting the stage for later chapters.

6. **Building Connection through Stories**: In their dialogue, while discussing the ongoing conflict, let them bond over shared experiences or mutual acquaintances. A hint of humor—perhaps laughing over a shared anecdote or cultural faux pas—can add depth to their budding relationship.

7. **Journey through History**: As Thomas delves deeper into Kyiv’s streets, let Natalia guide him through historical landmarks. Let her share personal anecdotes—like her childhood spent playing near the Saint Sophia's Cathedral—giving the landmarks a personal touch.

8. **City's Faces**: Their journey should introduce more characters: a street musician playing a haunting tune, a vendor selling trinkets related to local legends, or a mysterious figure whose path seems to cross theirs a tad too often.

9. **Kyiv’s Dual Nature**: Emphasize not just the beauty but the scars of conflict. A newly painted mural might cover bullet holes; a vibrant market might stand in the shadow of a war-torn relic.

10. **Thomas’s Revelations**: Through his interactions and observations, Thomas realizes the depth of Ukrainian resilience. He recalls other places he's been, recognizing the unique spirit of Kyiv.

11. **Foreshadowing the Mystery**: As they engage with the locals, perhaps Thomas comes across an odd trinket or hears a peculiar phrase repeatedly—a subtle hint at its importance in the larger mystery.

12. **Shared Mission**: Before parting, let their farewell be laden with a shared sense of purpose. Maybe Natalia hands Thomas a local artifact or a piece of information, emphasizing its significance.

13. **Underlying Suspense**: As Thomas walks away, introduce subtle hints of being watched—a fleeting shadow, a whisper, or maybe a brief, unsettling encounter with a stranger.

14. **The Unsolved Enigma**: Back at his temporary residence, as Thomas delves into Viktor Dmytrovich's files, end with a shocking revelation or discovery, compelling readers to move eagerly to the next chapter.

Remember, these beats are merely guiding lights; the beauty of Agatha Christie's style lies in the intricate weaving of characters, plot, and atmosphere. The details and layers you add will give your story its unique voice.
