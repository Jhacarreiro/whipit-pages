# The monastery — a revised passage

In the dim light of St. Michael's Golden-Domed Monastery, Thomas Harrington knelt before the flickering candles, his head bowed in prayer. The weight of past experiences pressed down on him, unbidden memories playing like a reel of film behind closed eyes.

Thomas muttered under his breath, "Lord, give me the courage to confront my demons and the knowledge to learn from them." A thick layer of serenity filled the church, the sturdy walls appearing to swallow up his imploring requests and bemoanings.

"Thomas?" A gentle voice snapped him from his meditation. He spun around to locate the source of the sound. Father Petrov stood in the corner with a face of worry.

"Father, I didn't realize you were here," Thomas uttered as he clambered to his feet.

"Your plea was quite heartfelt," Father Petrov mused. "What is it that concerns you?"

Thomas hesitated, then decided to confide in the man who had offered him spiritual guidance since his arrival in Kyiv. "My past weighs heavily upon me, Father. I've done things I'm not proud of, and the guilt never seems to fade away. I'm trying to be better, but it's hard to trust in myself that I can ever truly put the wrongs of my life behind me.

Father Petrov nodded thoughtfully. "Son, redemption is never a simple journey. Yet, it's imperative to bear in mind that absolution starts within. One must first forgive oneself before seeking forgiveness from others."

Before Thomas could respond, his mobile phone buzzed in his pocket, breaking the solemnity of the moment. Apologizing, he glanced at the text message and felt his heart skip a beat.

“I have to go, Father,” Thomas said, his words tumbling out in a rush. “It’s an emergency.”

Father Petrov crossed himself and said the blessing. “May God be with you, my son.” Thomas gave a quick nod before spinning away towards the door, his heart thundering in his chest.
