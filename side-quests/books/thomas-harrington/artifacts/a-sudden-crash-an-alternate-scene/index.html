A sudden crash — an alternate scene

AI draft. Surviving writing draft; alternative versions are retained.

Download text ↓

Kyiv, with the warm hum of the bistro fading behind him, Thomas felt an unexpected lightness. The evening's encounter with Marie had added an intriguing dimension to his time in this vibrant city. Their conversation was still fresh in his mind, an engaging mixture of shared laughter, insightful commentary, and the sparks of an undeniable chemistry.

He found himself walking along the Dnieper River, replaying parts of their conversation and quietly appreciating the city's nocturnal charm. The cityscape's twinkling lights reflected in the dark waters, creating a mesmerizing spectacle.

Just as he was about to cross the footbridge, a sudden, deafening crash echoed through the night. The tranquil scene was shattered by the violent screech of tires, followed by a thunderous collision. A plume of smoke erupted from a nearby street, where a vehicle had crashed into a lamppost. The post-accident silence was a stark contrast to the violent noise moments ago.

Thomas' heart pounded in his chest as a tide of panic washed over him. His breath came in sharp, rapid bursts, each more frantic than the last. His vision tunneled, and he could barely hear the concerned shouts and hurried footsteps of bystanders rushing towards the accident site. It was as if he were back in Chechnya, with the dreadful sounds of battle echoing all around him.

Marie, who had been walking a few steps behind him, quickly rushed to his side, her eyes wide with alarm. "Thomas, are you okay?" she asked, but her voice sounded muffled and distant.

"I... I..." Thomas stammered, struggling to form coherent sentences as the traumatic memories threatened to consume him. He could feel the icy grip of panic clutching at his heart, making it hard to breathe.

"What's wrong, Thomas?" Marie pressed, her face pale under the streetlights.

"I... was a soldier," he gasped, the words coming out in a desperate rush. "In Chechnya... the noise... it's like..."

Marie's eyes widened with understanding. "It's okay, Thomas," she said in a calm, steady voice, putting a reassuring hand on his arm. "You're safe now. It was just an accident. You're here in Kyiv, not in Chechnya."

Thomas nodded, trying to focus on her words. He took deep, shaky breaths, willing his heart rate to slow down. After what seemed like an eternity, the throbbing in his head began to subside, and the world around him started to come back into focus.

"I'm sorry, Marie," he said, his voice hoarse. "It's just... old memories."

Marie squeezed his arm gently, offering him a sympathetic smile. "You don't have to apologize, Thomas," she assured him. "Everyone has their demons. It's okay."

With Marie's help, Thomas managed to regain his composure. He thanked her quietly and they continued their walk in silence, the remnants of their lively conversation swallowed by the night.

As they neared his apartment, the city returned to its rhythmic hum, a comforting constant in the aftermath of the accident. The lights of Kyiv twinkled in the distance, the city seemingly unfazed by the evening's events.

Inside his apartment, Thomas found himself lost in thought. The pleasant memories of the evening were now marred by his panic attack, and the phantom sounds of a time long past. As he prepared for bed, the weight of his experiences felt heavy on his mind.

Tomorrow would be another day of diplomatic maneuvering and political intrigue. But tonight, he was reminded of his past, of the trauma that lingered in the corners of his mind. The city of Kyiv had proven

to be more than just a backdrop for his professional obligations; it was also a stage where his personal demons had come to play.

Despite everything, Thomas found himself looking forward to more days in Kyiv, to more evenings with Marie. There was something comforting about her presence, about her understanding. Maybe, he thought, she could help him face those demons.

With the sound of the rain lulling him to sleep, Thomas allowed himself to hope for a better tomorrow, for more peaceful evenings, and for a future in which the echoes of his past would not haunt him.
