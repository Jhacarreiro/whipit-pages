Chapter 1 — Sudowrite draft

Sudowrite working draft, dated 2023 by the author. Original prose and scene-beat markers are preserved; these are alternative versions of Chapter 1 in an unfinished novel. The repeated copy of this draft in the source document is included only once.

1 - 2

The first thing Thomas Harrington noticed as he stepped off the plane in Kyiv was a pair of worn-out brogues on an elderly man, just like his own. Instinctually, he took in the shoes of everyone around him—a habit from his detective days that had never quite left him. Tall and lean, with sharp features and piercing blue eyes, Thomas cut a striking figure as he strode purposefully through the airport, his own well-traveled brogues carrying him forward.

"Welcome to Kyiv," a voice crackled over the PA system, drawing Thomas's gaze upwards momentarily. But soon enough, his eyes were back to scanning the ground, searching for clues hidden in the simple act of walking.

Kyiv was a city of contrasts, its vibrant streets alive with the energy of life and commerce but also carrying the weight of conflict. As Thomas stood outside the airport, he could sense the two atmospheres coexisting uneasily, like oil and water refusing to mix. He watched as a shadow flitted across the sky overhead; it belonged to a military plane, barely discernible against the backdrop of clouds. In the window of a nearby shop, he caught sight of a hastily taken-down protest sign, the edges still curled from the adhesive.

"Excuse me, sir, do you need a taxi?" asked a young man, approaching Thomas.

"Ah, yes, thank you," Thomas replied, his thoughts momentarily interrupted. A short ride later, he found himself in the heart of the bustling metropolis. The sounds of laughter and conversation filled the air, mixed with the hum of car engines and clatter of footsteps on cobblestone streets.

Yet beneath this lively exterior, Thomas couldn't shake the feeling of tension that tugged at the edges of his mind. It seemed to echo in the distance, like the faint rumble of thunder before a storm.

"Something's not quite right," he muttered to himself as he exited the taxi and made his way towards his temporary residence. His eyes flitted from person to person, not missing a single pair of shoes.

"New city, new mystery," he thought as he adjusted his worn-out brogues and stepped inside.

3 - 4

As Thomas delved deeper into Kyiv's maze of streets, the city unfurled before him, an intricate tapestry woven from vibrant colors and sounds. Street vendors called out their wares, their voices melding with the sizzle of grilling meat and the rhythmic beat of a busker's drum. He stopped at one such vendor, curiosity piqued by the unusual aroma wafting from the stall. The man behind the counter gestured to a plate of steaming dumplings, smothered in a rich, tangy sauce.

"Varenyky," he said, smiling broadly. "Try some, friend."

Thomas hesitated, but eventually obliged, taking a tentative bite. The unfamiliar flavors danced on his tongue, leaving a lingering aftertaste that made him crave more. Yet even as he savored this novel experience, he couldn't help but feel like an intruder, an outsider in a world far removed from the polite teahouses and rain-slicked streets of London.

"Interesting," he mused, wiping his mouth with a napkin. "Quite different from what I'm used to."

"Ah, you are foreigner!" the vendor exclaimed, nodding sagely. "Enjoy your time in Kyiv, my friend."

"Thank you," Thomas replied, handing over a few bills before continuing on his way.

As he walked, he couldn't shake the feeling that he was being observed. It wasn't a sensation he was unfamiliar with, given his profession, but it gnawed at him nonetheless. He scanned the crowd, noting each face that passed by, searching for any sign of recognition or ill intent.

"Excuse me," a voice called out, startling him from his thoughts.

He turned and found himself face to face with a woman. She was petite, with short dark hair and a determined set to her jaw. Ink stains smudged her fingers, and she stood with the well-worn yet proud posture of someone who had seen much but refused to be bowed by it. A distinctive pendant hung from her neck, its design reminiscent of Ukrainian folklore.

"Are you Thomas Harrington?" she asked, her eyes scrutinizing him.

"Who's asking?" he replied cautiously, his mind racing through possible scenarios.

"My name is Natalia Ivanova," she said, extending a hand. "I'm a journalist."

"Ah." His gaze lingered on her ink-stained fingers, and he shook her hand firmly. "What can I do for you, Ms. Ivanova?"

"Call me Natalia, please," she insisted, her voice tinged with warmth. "I believe we have some common interests, especially concerning recent events in our city."

"Is that so?" Thomas mused, his curiosity piqued. He looked at her intently, wondering how this woman, a stranger in a foreign land, had managed to find him among the throngs of people that filled Kyiv's streets.

"Indeed," Natalia confirmed, the faintest hint of a smile playing on her lips. "But this isn't the place to talk. Walk with me."

"Very well," Thomas conceded, falling into step beside her, his senses sharpening as they ventured deeper into the heart of Kyiv together.

5 - 6

As they meandered through the crowded streets, Natalia recounted snippets of stories she had covered over the years. "There was this one time," she began, "during a particularly intense airstrike, when I heard something that still haunts me to this day." Her voice lowered to a hushed tone as they passed a group of laughing children, oblivious to their conversation. "A family was trapped inside their home, and the mother sang a lullaby to her child to calm them amidst the chaos."

"Remarkable," Thomas murmured, his eyes fixed on the ground as he absorbed the weight of her words. He imagined the scene – the sound of explosions, the cries of desperation, and the gentle voice of a mother trying to protect her child from the harsh reality outside.

"Ukraine is filled with such stories," Natalia continued, her gaze sweeping across the faces of those around them. "And not all are tragedies. There's an urban legend about a spectral black dog who roams the city at night, guarding its people from evil spirits." She smiled wryly at Thomas's raised eyebrow. "Perhaps it's just a comforting tale for children, but in times like these, we need all the hope we can get."

Thomas chuckled lightly, appreciating the warmth behind her words. "In my line of work, sometimes you'll find that truth is stranger than fiction."

"Ah, so you've encountered your fair share of peculiar cases, I presume?" Natalia asked, a playful glint in her eyes.

"More than you could imagine," Thomas admitted, recalling past adventures that seemed almost fantastical in retrospect. "I remember one case where a man's prized collection of antique shoes went missing, only to be found hidden in plain sight."

"Really?" Natalia laughed, her eyes crinkling at the corners. "Sounds like a true crime classic."

"Indeed," Thomas agreed, his lips curving into a genuine smile. "But let's return to the subject at hand –" He nodded towards a group of protesters marching in the distance, their voices raised in unison. "The ongoing conflict here is something I'd like to better understand."

Natalia nodded solemnly, her expression shifting as she focused on the serious topic. "It's a complicated situation, but we are a resilient people. We've faced adversity before, and we'll face it again."

As they spoke of the hardships and turmoil that plagued Ukraine, Thomas found himself drawn to Natalia's passion and determination. Despite the grim reality of their conversation, they shared a connection through their experiences and mutual desire for justice.

"Listen, Thomas," Natalia said, pausing beside an old church, its walls scarred by the passage of time. "I know our acquaintance is brief, but I believe we can help each other. Together, we might uncover truths that would remain hidden otherwise."

"Agreed," Thomas replied, extending his hand. As they shook hands, he felt a newfound sense of camaraderie, a partnership forged amidst the vibrant chaos of Kyiv.

7 - 8

"Come, let's walk this way," Natalia said, gesturing towards a narrow cobblestone street that wound its way between ancient buildings. Thomas followed her lead, his eyes taking in the historic beauty of Kyiv as they delved deeper into its heart.

"See that tower?" Natalia pointed to a tall, ornate structure rising above the nearby rooftops. "That's part of Saint Sophia's Cathedral. I used to play hide-and-seek with my friends there when I was a child. It was our little secret oasis amidst the bustling city."

A wistful smile played on her lips as she shared her memories, and Thomas couldn't help but feel a sense of warmth towards her. She brought the city to life in a way that no guidebook ever could.

As they continued their journey, the faces of Kyiv revealed themselves. A street musician stood on a corner, his fingers expertly plucking the strings of a bandura, the haunting melody weaving through the air like an invisible thread connecting the city's past to its present.

They passed a vendor selling trinkets, each piece unique and steeped in local legends. Thomas paused for a moment, examining a small wooden carving of a mythical creature said to protect homes from evil spirits.

"Ah, the domovoy," Natalia explained, watching him with interest. "Many Ukrainians still believe in these household guardians. They say that if you treat them well, they will ward off misfortune."

"Interesting," Thomas mused, running his fingers over the intricately carved figure before returning it to the vendor with a nod of appreciation.

As they walked on, Thomas couldn't shake the nagging sensation that they were being watched. He caught glimpses of a mysterious figure, always at the edge of his vision – never close enough to identify, yet never too far away.

"Is something the matter?" Natalia asked, noticing his unease.

"Perhaps it's just my imagination," Thomas replied, forcing a smile. "But I have the strangest feeling that we're not alone."

Natalia glanced around, her gaze sharp and searching. "It's possible," she admitted, her voice low. "Kyiv is a city of many layers, and not all of them are friendly to outsiders. But do not worry; I will keep you safe."

"Thank you," Thomas said, touched by her concern. They continued their exploration of Kyiv's rich history, side by side, forging a bond amidst the city's ever-shifting landscape. And though the shadowy figure remained a constant presence in the periphery, Thomas couldn't help but feel that, with Natalia beside him, they would uncover the truth together – one step at a time.

9 - 10

The golden sun dipped below the horizon as Natalia led Thomas through a narrow alley, the cobblestones beneath their feet worn smooth by generations of footsteps. They emerged at the edge of a vibrant market, its colorful stalls and bustling crowd painting a lively scene in stark contrast to the war-torn relic that loomed just beyond – a crumbling building, its facade pocked with bullet holes and darkened by fire.

"Kyiv is a city of contrasts," Natalia said, her voice tinged with both pride and sadness. "We have suffered much, but we also celebrate life and our love for this land."

As they wandered deeper into the market, Thomas was struck by a newly painted mural adorning the side of a building. The vivid colors seemed to dance across the wall, telling a story of hope and resilience as they masked the scars of conflict beneath.

"Your people are strong," he observed, unable to tear his gaze from the powerful image. "I've been to many places in my life, but Kyiv... there's something special about this city."

"Thank you," Natalia replied, her eyes shining with gratitude. "We do not forget our past, but we also look forward – to a brighter future."

A sudden gust of wind carried with it the lilting melody of a street musician's violin, the haunting notes weaving through the air like a whispered prayer. Thomas felt a shiver run down his spine as the music resonated deep within him, echoing the strength and determination he sensed in the people around him.

"Thomas," Natalia said, her voice barely audible above the din of the market, "I want you to meet someone." She led him to an elderly woman sitting on a low stool, her gnarled hands deftly threading beads onto a length of wire.

"Marusya here makes some of the most beautiful jewelry I've ever seen," Natalia explained, her voice filled with respect. "Each piece tells a story – of our history, our hopes, and our dreams."

Thomas picked up a delicate necklace, the intricate patterns of silver wirework cradling a vibrant blue stone that seemed to shimmer with an inner light. "It's exquisite," he breathed.

"Ah, that is синьо-океанічний камінь – the Blue Ocean Stone," Marusya said, a smile creasing her weathered face. "We believe it brings strength and courage to those who wear it."

"Perhaps I could use some of that," Thomas mused, thinking of the task ahead and the mysterious figure that haunted his peripheral vision.

"Then let it be a reminder," Natalia urged, her hand resting on his arm, "that you are not alone in this journey. We may come from different worlds, but we share a common purpose – to uncover the truth and bring justice to those who seek to harm us."

As they left the market behind, Thomas reflected on the unique spirit of Kyiv and its people – their resilience and determination, their ability to find beauty amidst the scars of conflict. And though the road ahead was uncertain, he knew he had forged a bond with Natalia that would carry them through whatever challenges lay in wait.

11 - 12

As Thomas and Natalia continued their journey through Kyiv, they wandered into a narrow cobblestone alley lined with quaint little shops. The scent of freshly baked bread mingled with the sharp tang of pickles, while melodic folk songs drifted through the air.

"сила в єдності," murmured an old man as he shuffled past them, his eyes twinkling with a knowing smile.

"Strength in unity," Natalia translated for Thomas, her brow creasing slightly. "It's an old Ukrainian saying. I've heard it quite often lately."

"Interesting," Thomas mused, filing the phrase away in his mind as they continued on.

Their path led them to an antique shop, its windows filled with weathered books and glinting trinkets. A small silver figurine caught Thomas's eye – a figure shrouded in robes, holding aloft a lantern that seemed to cast a shadow rather than light.

"Curious," he murmured, picking it up carefully. "What do you make of this, Natalia?"

She studied the figurine, her eyes narrowing. "I'm not sure. It's not something I've seen before, but there is something... familiar about it."

"Perhaps it's connected to the Blue Ocean Stone," Thomas suggested, remembering the necklace from the market. He couldn't shake the feeling that these seemingly innocuous items held some deeper significance.

They left the antique shop, their conversation meandering back to the ongoing conflict and their mutual mission. As they shared stories of their experiences, Thomas was struck by how much they had in common despite their different backgrounds.

"Before we part ways, I want to give you something," Natalia said, reaching into her pocket. She produced a small envelope, sealed with a wax stamp bearing the image of the lantern-bearing figure.

"Inside are some notes I've made about the case," she explained, pressing the envelope into Thomas's hand. "I hope they'll be of help to you."

"Thank you, Natalia," he replied sincerely, feeling the weight of their shared responsibility settle on his shoulders. As they embraced in farewell, he knew that whatever lay ahead, they would face it together.

"Remember, Thomas," Natalia whispered, her voice tinged with both sadness and resolve, "сила в єдності – strength in unity. We're stronger together than apart."

With the envelope tucked safely into his jacket pocket and the mysterious phrase echoing in his mind, Thomas could not help but feel that the key to unraveling the mystery lay somewhere within the very heart of Kyiv itself. And as he prepared to walk this path, he was grateful for the bond he had formed with Natalia, certain that it would guide them through the shadows and uncertainties that awaited them.

13 - 14

Thomas walked away from Natalia, feeling the weight of their shared purpose and the envelope tucked safely inside his jacket pocket. The cobblestone streets seemed to echo with whispers of the past, and each step felt heavier than the last. As he turned a corner, Thomas noticed a fleeting shadow disappearing around the next bend. He couldn't shake the nagging sensation that someone was watching him.

"Must be my imagination," he muttered under his breath, looking down at his worn-out brogues as they tapped against the stones. But even as he tried to dismiss it, he couldn't help but feel the hair on the back of his neck stand on end.

"Excuse me, sir," a stranger's voice called out, startling Thomas and causing him to jump slightly. He turned to see an elderly man standing just behind him, his wrinkled face creased with concern. "Are you lost?"

"No, thank you," Thomas replied, forcing a smile and hoping his unease didn't show. "I'm just heading back to my lodgings."

"Ah, I see," the man said, nodding sagely. "Be careful, young man. There are many things in this city that are not what they seem." With that cryptic warning, the man shuffled away, leaving Thomas even more unsettled.

"Odd fellow," Thomas thought, picking up his pace as he continued toward his temporary residence.

Once inside, Thomas locked the door behind him and set to work examining the contents of the envelope Natalia had given him. He spread the papers across the small table by the window, trying to make sense of the notes and photographs. They were all connected to Viktor Dmytrovich, the imposing military officer whose motives remained murky at best.

"сила в єдності" Thomas murmured, remembering Natalia's whispered words. He knew he needed to find the connection between Viktor and the mysterious phrase. As he delved deeper into the files, his eyes were drawn to a photograph of Viktor standing in front of an old building, its walls scarred by conflict.

"Curious," Thomas mused, leaning in for a closer look. He noticed a small emblem on the building's facade, just above Viktor's shoulder – a lantern-bearing figure. The same figure that adorned the wax seal on Natalia's envelope.

"Strength in unity" indeed, Thomas thought, his heart pounding with excitement as he began to see the beginnings of a pattern weaving through the notes and images. Whatever this enigma was, it was bigger than he had initially realized, and it seemed to be rooted in the very core of Kyiv itself.

"Viktor... what are you hiding?" he whispered to himself, feeling both exhilarated and apprehensive. It was clear that there was more to this mystery than met the eye, and Thomas knew he would need all of his wits – and Natalia's help – to unravel the truth.

As thunder rumbled in the distance and rain began to patter against the windowpane, Thomas Harrington steeled himself for the challenges that lay ahead, his determination fueled by the shocking revelation he had discovered. And as he dove deeper into the tangled web of secrets, he could only hope that he and Natalia would emerge from the shadows unscathed, their bond stronger for having faced the darkness together.
