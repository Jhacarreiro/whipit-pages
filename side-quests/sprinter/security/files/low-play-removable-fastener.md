# Low play in a removable fastener

20 May 2026 · Mechanical design study

The brief was a compact removable fastening with as little play as possible. Listing anchor fittings did not answer the main question: what should connect the parts, take up slack and remain convenient to remove?

The comparison developed around four mechanical families:

| Concept | Question to resolve |
|---|---|
| Adjustable lever fastening | Can adjustment and repeated operation remain separate, so the fit does not need resetting each time? |
| Retained pin connection | Can the parts align consistently while keeping clearance small? |
| Threaded link or tensioner | Is fine adjustment worth the additional opening and closing steps? |
| Flexible link with a tensioning mechanism | Which component provides the connection, and which removes the slack? |

A further correction separated tightness from retention. A connection could feel taut while still needing a deliberate retaining action. That added a distinct requirement to the comparison instead of treating low play as sufficient by itself.

The cable alternative exposed another distinction: changing the linking material would not automatically solve the tensioning problem. The AI compared steel cable and synthetic rope arrangements, alongside rigid fittings, and found concrete examples of the proposed component families.

The result was a more precise design brief covering adjustment, retention, repeated use and release. The final rope-based arrangement was an AI proposal, with no subsequent confirmation of a selected design, fabricated part or load test. This remains a record of the design exploration.
