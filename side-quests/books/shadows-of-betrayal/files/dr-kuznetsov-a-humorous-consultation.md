# Dr. Kuznetsov — a humorous consultation

Picture the scene: a snug, book-lined office with not just one, but two Freudian couches. Stacks of thick, scholarly tomes teetered precariously in one corner, while in another, a hat-stand bravely bore the weight of an array of outlandishly large Ukrainian fur hats. A musty smell of old books and overworked leather chairs hung in the air, mildly challenged by the faint scent of a lavender air freshener that had long since given up its prime.

Dr. Ivan Kuznetsov, a psychiatrist with an unnerving penchant for dramatically swirling his coat around him before seating himself, and an equally unsettling preference for staring at people over the rim of his half-moon glasses, sat perched behind a gigantic mahogany desk.

Seated on the other side of this desk was Thomas Harrington. The tension was palpable, but Thomas chose to ease it with his signature British nonchalance and a slight dash of humor.

"I must say, Ivan," Thomas began, looking around the room with a bemused smile. "With all these Freudian couches, one might think you were planning to psychoanalyze an army!"

Ivan chuckled, his stern demeanor momentarily breaking. "Ah, Thomas, always quick with a quip. But you must understand, this is Ukraine. We believe in always being prepared!"

Their laughter subsided, and the room once again sank into an atmosphere of tense expectation. Thomas cleared his throat, subtly shifting the mood.

"Ivan, I need your advice," he began, his tone now serious. "Something is brewing in Kyiv, like a pot of your infamous borsch."

Ivan raised an eyebrow, intrigued by the sudden shift. "Go on," he said, leaning back and tenting his fingers.

Thomas spent the next hour divulging his findings, recounting the veiled conversations, hidden signals, and ominous glances. Ivan listened attentively, his expression unreadable behind his glasses.

"An increase in military budget, a potentially significant tech-energy collaboration, and not to forget, the secretive woman at the party," Thomas finished, a note of urgency creeping into his voice. "All these are unrelated on the surface, but my gut tells me otherwise."

Ivan mulled over what Thomas had shared. "Ah, your infamous gut! The gut that managed to predict the fall of the Berlin Wall!" Ivan chuckled, adjusting his glasses. "Your gut has more credibility than some of our intelligence agencies!"

Thomas, suppressing a grin, pointed out, "Humor aside, Ivan, the situation could be grave. We must figure out the ties that bind these seemingly unrelated incidents."
