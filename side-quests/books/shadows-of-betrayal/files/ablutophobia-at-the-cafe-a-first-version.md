# Ablutophobia at the café — a first version

Her words hung in the air, a playful challenge. Thomas took a moment, swirling the amber liquid in his glass. His eyes met hers, a flicker of amusement in their depths. "You could say I have a penchant for adventure, Marie," he began, his voice a low rumble. "And of course, the company is an added bonus."

A light chuckle escaped her lips, her eyes sparkling with mirth. "Oh, a charmer, aren't we, Thomas?" she teased, raising her glass in a mock toast.

Their playful banter was cut short by the sudden arrival of a waiter, a tray balanced expertly in his hand. "Your pastries, Madam, Sir," he announced, placing a delectable array of French pastries on their table.

Thomas's gaze landed on the pastries, a momentary flicker of discomfort crossing his features. Marie, with her keen observation, did not miss the change. "Is everything alright, Thomas?" she asked, her gaze softening.

Thomas cleared his throat, trying to regain his composure. "Yes, everything's fine. I just... I have a bit of an unusual condition."

Marie's brows furrowed in concern. "What kind of condition?"

He hesitated for a moment, then decided to let her in on his secret. After all, secrets had a way of bringing people closer. "I have Ablutophobia," he admitted, his voice barely a whisper.

Her eyes widened slightly in surprise. "Ablutophobia? That's... a fear of bathing, isn't it?"

Thomas chuckled, a touch of embarrassment in his eyes. "Yes, it is. But it's not just bathing. It's a fear of anything related to cleaning. And right now," he gestured towards the immaculate pastries, "I can't help but think of how meticulously they must have been prepared, how clean everything must be. It's a bit overwhelming."

Marie looked at him, her eyes filled with understanding. She reached across the table, placing her hand on his in a comforting gesture. "I can't begin to imagine how difficult that must be for you, Thomas," she said softly. "But it's okay. We all have our quirks. And if you're not comfortable, we don't have to stay."

The genuine empathy in her words touched Thomas. He gave her a grateful smile, feeling a wave of warmth wash over him. "Thank you, Marie. I appreciate that."

As they left the cafe, arm in arm, Thomas couldn't help but feel a sense of relief. Marie had not only accepted his unusual phobia but had also offered him comfort. And in that moment, amidst the buzzing streets of Kyiv, Thomas felt a sense of connection he hadn't experienced in a long time.
