# The premise brainstorm

## Key points

1. **Setting**: The story could be set in Kyiv, the capital of Ukraine, during the early days of the invasion. The city's atmosphere is tense and uncertain, with everyone on edge due to the ongoing conflict.

2. **Main Characters**: The protagonist could be a British diplomat stationed in Ukraine, somewhat reminiscent of Christie's less-known character, Colonel Race. This diplomat is also a secret intelligence agent, with keen observation skills and a passion for justice. There could also be a variety of other characters, including Ukrainian citizens, Russian soldiers, and international reporters, each with their own distinct personalities and secrets.

3. **The Crime**: In the midst of the chaos, a high-ranking official is found dead under suspicious circumstances. It's initially assumed to be a casualty of war, but our diplomat protagonist notices some inconsistencies and starts investigating it as a murder.

4. **Investigation**: The investigation takes place against the backdrop of the invasion. The protagonist has to navigate not just the usual challenges of solving a murder, but also the additional complications posed by the war: curfews, restricted movement, and heightened suspicion among everyone.

5. **Clues and Red Herrings**: True to Christie's style, there would be a number of clues laid out, some of which would be red herrings. The truth could be skillfully hidden among lies, half-truths, and misinformation, with the chaos of war making it even more difficult to distinguish fact from fiction.

6. **Resolution**: Eventually, the diplomat manages to unravel the truth. The murder could be tied to a conspiracy or a secret that the victim was about to expose, one that could change the course of the war. The murderer could be an unexpected character, with motives rooted in personal gain, patriotism, or a twisted sense of justice.

7. **Themes**: In addition to the mystery, the novel could explore themes like the moral complexities of war, the resilience of people in the face of adversity, and the question of what justice means in a time of conflict.

## Locations

1. **Lviv**: Located in western Ukraine, Lviv is known for its beautiful and historic city center. The protagonist could travel here to meet with a contact, gather information, or follow a lead related to the murder case.

2. **Donetsk**: This city in eastern Ukraine has been a significant location in the real-world conflict between Ukraine and Russia. A visit here could reveal more about the tensions leading up to the invasion and provide context for the larger political situation.

3. **Chernobyl**: The site of the infamous nuclear disaster could serve as an atmospheric and symbolic location. The protagonist might go there in search of a clue or a person connected to the case.

4. **Crimea**: The peninsula, which has been a contentious region between Ukraine and Russia, could be another important location for the story. It could be the site of a pivotal event or revelation in the investigation.

5. **Moscow, Russia**: The investigation might lead the protagonist to the heart of Russia itself. This could provide an opportunity to explore the other side of the conflict and introduce new characters and perspectives.

6. **International Locations**: Depending on the scope of the conspiracy or secret involved in the murder, the protagonist could also visit other countries, perhaps to trace the movements of the victim or the suspected murderer, or to liaise with other intelligence agencies. This could include cities like London, Paris, or even Washington D.C.

## Challenges

1. **War Conditions**: The ongoing conflict would pose a constant threat. This could include danger from bombings, gunfire, and other forms of violence. The protagonist would need to navigate these hazards while pursuing leads and suspects.

2. **Restricted Movement**: War often brings restrictions on movement, such as curfews and checkpoints. This could make it difficult for the protagonist to access certain areas or meet with key individuals.

3. **Limited Resources**: Depending on the circumstances, the protagonist might have limited access to typical resources like forensic technology, reliable communication systems, or even basic necessities like electricity and safe shelter.

4. **Language Barrier**: If the protagonist is a British diplomat, there could be language barriers when interacting with locals or deciphering important documents or clues.

5. **Political Tensions**: Navigating the political landscape would be challenging. The protagonist might face resistance or even active sabotage from various factions, each with their own agendas.

6. **Trust Issues**: In a time of war, people tend to be more suspicious and less trusting, particularly towards foreigners. This could hinder the protagonist's ability to gather information or form alliances.

7. **Psychological Stress**: The stress of being in a war zone, combined with the pressure of solving a murder, could take a psychological toll on the protagonist. They might struggle with feelings of fear, guilt, or helplessness, and these emotions could influence their decisions and actions.

8. **Deteriorating Situation**: As the invasion progresses, the situation could become increasingly unstable and unpredictable, with new challenges arising unexpectedly.

## The crime

The crime at the center of this story could involve high-level political or military figures to underline its potential to impact the course of the war. Here's one possible scenario:

The victim could be a high-ranking Ukrainian military officer who was leading the defense against the Russian invasion. His death is initially assumed to be the result of a Russian attack, but our protagonist, the British diplomat, finds evidence of foul play, suggesting that the officer was in fact murdered.

The officer was known to be a brilliant strategist, and his loss is a severe blow to the Ukrainian resistance. However, as the diplomat delves deeper into the case, he uncovers that the officer had been working on a secret plan that could potentially turn the tide of the war in favor of Ukraine.

Perhaps this plan involves a highly strategic military maneuver, the exposure of a double agent within the Russian ranks, or the revelation of a secret alliance with another country. The officer had kept the plan a closely guarded secret, sharing it with only a few trusted individuals.

The motive for the murder could then be tied to this secret plan. Perhaps the murderer wanted to prevent the plan from being implemented, or they wanted to steal the plan for their own purposes. This could point the finger at a range of suspects, from Russian spies to traitorous Ukrainian officials, or even third-party actors who stand to gain from the continuation of the war.

In this scenario, solving the murder becomes about more than just bringing a killer to justice. It's also about discovering and implementing the officer's secret plan, which could potentially change the course of the war. It adds an extra layer of tension and urgency to the protagonist's investigation, and raises the stakes of the story considerably.

## The resolution

After a series of twists and turns, the diplomat uncovers that the murderer is a high-ranking official within the Ukrainian government who is secretly working for the Russians. This official had discovered the military officer's plan and killed him to prevent its implementation, intending to pass the details of the plan to the Russians.

The diplomat confronts the official in a tense showdown, during which the official tries to justify their actions, arguing that they believed a Russian victory was inevitable and they were trying to save lives by preventing further fighting. The diplomat counters that it's not their decision to make, and that the Ukrainian people have a right to fight for their independence.

Following this confrontation, the official is arrested and the diplomat manages to recover the details of the secret plan. He passes this information onto the remaining Ukrainian leaders, who implement the plan successfully, leading to a major victory that shifts the momentum of the war in Ukraine's favor.

However, the war is not over, and the diplomat knows there are likely more challenges ahead. The novel ends on a hopeful but uncertain note, reflecting the complex reality of war.

## Clues and red herrings

1. **Clues:**
    - **Unusual Behaviour**: A particular character might act suspiciously before or after the murder, drawing the protagonist's attention. This could be anything from being unusually nervous, changing their routine, trying to alter their appearance, or showing an unusual interest in the investigation.
    - **Physical Evidence**: The protagonist could find a unique item at the crime scene that doesn't belong there, like a piece of a foreign uniform, a specific type of ammunition not used by Ukrainians, or a coded message.
    - **Witness Accounts**: Witnesses might provide information about unusual happenings or people they observed near the crime scene around the time of the murder.
    - **Discrepancies**: Inconsistencies in the alibis of the suspects, or discrepancies between the crime scene and the initial assumptions about the murder, could provide important clues.

2. **Red Herrings:**
    - **Misleading Evidence**: A planted piece of evidence could lead the protagonist to suspect someone who is actually innocent. For example, a personal item belonging to a seemingly suspicious character could be found at the crime scene, but it might later be revealed that this item was stolen and planted to mislead the investigation.
    - **False Confessions or Accusations**: A character could confess to the crime or accuse someone else in an attempt to draw attention away from the real murderer.
    - **Irrelevant Secrets**: Characters might be hiding secrets that, while intriguing, are not directly related to the crime. These secrets could distract the protagonist and the readers, leading them down false paths.
    - **Misinterpretation of Clues**: Some clues might be open to multiple interpretations, and the protagonist (and the reader) might initially interpret them in a way that leads them in the wrong direction.

## Themes

1. **Betrayal and Loyalty**: The concept of betrayal would be central, given that the murder involves a high-ranking official working for the enemy. This could be juxtaposed with examples of loyalty and patriotism from other characters.

2. **The Moral Ambiguity of War**: The story could explore the gray areas of morality in war, such as the question of whether ends justify means. The traitor might argue that they were trying to save lives by preventing further fighting, raising questions about the nature of good and evil in wartime.

3. **The Human Cost of War**: The protagonist would witness the impact of war on everyday people, highlighting the human cost of political and military conflicts.

4. **Power and Corruption**: The story could delve into the ways in which power can be abused, with characters exploiting the chaos of war for their own ends.

5. **Courage and Resilience**: Despite the challenges and dangers of the war, characters would demonstrate courage and resilience, reinforcing the human capacity to endure and resist in the face of adversity.

6. **Truth and Deception**: As with any mystery, the search for truth would be a primary theme, with the protagonist navigating a web of lies and deception to uncover the reality of the murder.

7. **Duty and Sacrifice**: Characters may be required to make significant sacrifices for their duties, raising questions about the costs and obligations of service to one's country.
