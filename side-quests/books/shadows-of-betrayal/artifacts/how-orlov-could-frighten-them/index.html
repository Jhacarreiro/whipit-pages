How Orlov could frighten them

AI draft. Surviving writing draft; alternative versions are retained.

Download text ↓

The Agatha Christie style of storytelling often employs clever misdirection, red herrings, and multi-layered mysteries to keep readers engaged. Given Dmitri Orlov's appearance at the end of Chapter 6, the tense atmosphere, and the fact that he's watching Thomas and Natalia, it's clear he's planning something.

In Christie's style, this could take a number of forms. Orlov might employ fear as a weapon, and the following strategies could align with that:

  1. Direct Threats: Orlov could leave anonymous threats to scare off Thomas and Natalia. This could include threats of physical harm, threats against their loved ones, or threats to ruin their reputations or careers.

  2. Misinformation: He could attempt to mislead their investigation by planting false evidence or spreading rumors. This could potentially send them off on a tangent, wasting their time and resources, or make them question the validity of their own evidence.

  3. Sabotage: Dmitri could stage incidents to throw off their investigation. This could range from break-ins where evidence is stolen or tampered with, to more extreme events like arson or orchestrated 'accidents'.

  4. Unnerving Incidents: To sow fear, Orlov could arrange for unnerving incidents to happen around Thomas and Natalia. Strange phone calls, being followed, having their homes broken into, finding personal items displaced or missing, or even directly confronting them with disguised or anonymous figures, could all be used to create an atmosphere of fear and paranoia.

  5. Use of Third Parties: Orlov could involve innocent third parties in his plans to throw them off the scent or to scare them. This might involve using people as pawns to make it seem like they are the ones behind the illegal activities, effectively creating a 'red herring' for them to chase.

In any of these scenarios, the main goal would be to scare Thomas and Natalia enough to make them drop their investigation, or at least to slow them down, all while keeping Orlov's involvement hidden. The nature of his plan would ultimately depend on his character - whether he's inclined to more subtle or more drastic actions.
