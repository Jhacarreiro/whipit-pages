# A consultation that keeps the cause hidden

1

The chime of the grandfather clock echoed through the room, its melody a subtle reminder of the passage of time. As its notes faded, Thomas found himself studying the tapestry of his own reflection in the room's mirrored wall. His steel-blue eyes stared back at him, weary yet resilient, framed by a pair of well-worn glasses.

"Do you remember the first time you felt it, Thomas?" Dr. Kuznetsov's gentle question cut through the silence. The older man leaned forward, his keen gaze never leaving Thomas.

Thomas, his hands clasped tightly, nodded, swallowing hard. "Yes," he answered simply, his voice betraying a hint of the unease that had been gnawing at him for some time.

Kuznetsov, a seasoned psychiatrist, noted the hesitation. This was an old dance, and he had seen many partners. He waited patiently, his years of experience guiding his response. "And what does it feel like when it happens?" He asked, a comforting note of understanding in his voice.

2

Describing it was always difficult. The feeling was so visceral, so intensely personal, that verbalizing it always seemed to diminish its true magnitude. "It feels... constricting, suffocating. Like I'm being swallowed by the earth and there's no way out," Thomas managed, his words painting an unnerving picture.

"I see," Dr. Kuznetsov murmured, taking note of the vivid description. He took a moment, observing Thomas closely. "But you're managing it, aren't you?"

Thomas looked up, met Dr. Kuznetsov's gaze. "I'm trying," he said, his voice filled with a quiet determination. "I'm fighting."

"And that's all you can do, Thomas," Dr. Kuznetsov said gently. "Remember, you're not alone in this."

3

As the session wore on, the psychiatrist made several insightful observations, encouraging Thomas to delve deeper into his experience. Yet, he kept a respectful distance from the root cause of Thomas's condition, understanding that certain areas might still be too painful to explore.

By the time the session ended, Thomas felt drained but in an odd way, relieved. These sessions were never easy, yet he was grateful for Dr. Kuznetsov's unwavering support.

With a polite nod to the doctor, Thomas rose from the comfortable leather chair. It was time to return to his other life – his real world. The world where he was not just Thomas, the man grappling with an invisible tormentor, but Thomas Anderson, the top cybersecurity specialist at the British embassy in Moscow.
