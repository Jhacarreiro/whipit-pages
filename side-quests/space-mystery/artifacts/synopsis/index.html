Synopsis

31 January 2024 · 09:21·Draft for review

Download this document ↓

In a near-future world where space travel is common, driven by both geopolitical motives and corporate interests, a Mars mission becomes the backdrop for an intense psychological thriller. At the centre of the story is Alex, a brilliant scientist whose mental health deteriorates during the mission. As Alex's breakdown leads to paranoia and delusions, causing disruptive and dangerous behaviour, the other crew members must manage the crisis while dealing with the mission’s challenges.

The crew includes a mix of nationalities and roles, from mission commanders to engineers and liaisons, reflecting global interests in space exploration. Captain Priya Singh, an experienced astronaut from India, is known for her calm demeanour and strong leadership skills. Dr Leo Hernandez, an American engineer on board, plays a key role in troubleshooting the technical issues that arise and is initially sceptical of Alex's behaviour. Elena Kozlova, a Russian communications specialist responsible for maintaining contact with Earth, becomes an ally to Alex, sympathising with their plight but also hiding a secret related to the mission. Dr Yuto Takahashi, a Japanese biologist conducting important research on the spacecraft, provides critical insights into unravelling the mysteries aboard the ship. Dr Nadia Buhari, a Ghanaian medical doctor tasked with ensuring the crew's physical and mental well-being, plays a pivotal role in diagnosing and treating Alex's condition.

As Alex's psychological state deteriorates, they become obsessed with a territorial dispute over Mars' Elysium Sector, drawing connections between their mission and the larger geopolitical game at play. This adds another layer of paranoia and urgency to their actions. Zara Al-Farsi, a UAE government official and liaison for the mission representing the interests of the international consortium funding the mission, becomes actively involved in trying to mediate the conflict from aboard the spacecraft. The conflict on Mars mirrors the tensions experienced within the spacecraft, highlighting the themes of international cooperation and competition in space.

In this future world, India and China have emerged as dominant players in space exploration, reflecting their growing economic and technological prowess on Earth. This shift is a result of their large, young, and increasingly skilled populations, alongside significant investments in space technology. The subplot could explore the rivalry between India and China in space, mirroring their competition for economic and technological supremacy on Earth. This includes a race to claim valuable resources on Mars and the Moon. There could also be elements of cooperation, such as joint missions or shared space stations, reflecting the complex interdependence of these global powers.

The novel explores the ethics of long-term space missions and the treatment of astronauts, delving into the mental effects of isolation in space and the responsibilities of those who send humans on such missions. It also questions whether humanity can overcome its divisive tendencies in the face of new frontiers. By integrating these subplots, the novel becomes a commentary on contemporary global issues projected onto the vast canvas of interplanetary exploration.

The resolution could involve a critical event, such as a natural disaster on Mars or a technological failure at one of the disputed bases, forcing the conflicting parties to cooperate for survival. This event could lead to a temporary resolution of the conflict and set the stage for future cooperation, or it could deepen the rift, setting up further plot development in potential sequels.
